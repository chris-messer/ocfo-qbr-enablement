05 Client need

Databricks supports SOX-compliant workloads; no cloud provider holds a SOX certification

Databricks · compliance positioningstandard response · verbatim

Can Databricks be used with SOX compliance?

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 is a US federal law administered by the Securities and Exchange Commission. Databricks can support a SOX-compliant workload, and has customers running SOX-compliant workloads on the platform.

Most SOX requirements are about how customers maintain their source code and their testing processes — commonly Repos for source control and cluster policies for allowed instance types and runtime versions.

When it comes to actual audits, there is no SOX certification or validation for cloud service providers. Databricks is a general-purpose, data-agnostic compute platform and is not audited for SOX. For review of Databricks itself, consult the controls within our SOC 2 Type II report covering change management, release management, and access management.

Loses credibility instantly
  • “Databricks is SOX certified.”
  • “SOX does not apply to Genie.”
  • “Do not worry about Internal Audit.”
  • Ask for SOC 2 Type II when they want evidence about our controls — that request path is real and answerable.

17 Appendix

Lead with scorecard units — days, dollars, basis points — not platform nouns

If the sentence cannot land on a P&L or balance-sheet effect, it is not ready for this buyer.

DSO

Days to collect after a sale. A 10-day cut at $1B revenue ≈ $27M working capital.

DPO

Days to pay suppliers. Within terms, higher preserves cash; P2P intelligence targets 5–10 days.

CCC

Cash conversion cycle: DSO + inventory days − DPO. Shorter means less cash trapped in operations.

EBIT

Operating profit the board watches. Every use case should name its EBIT path: cost, revenue, or capital.

bps

Basis points — 100 bps = 1.0%. Use for forecast accuracy, margin, and cost moves.

Run-rate

Annualize a period win. A $5M quarter is a $20M run-rate when you present ROI.

Framing that lands: “AI yes, with controls.” The CEO already committed to AI; the CFO has to fund it, govern it, and defend it to the board — with attributable offsets, not a slide deck.

ERP and EPM stay as systems of record; Databricks is the governed intelligence layer

Field-guide posture that pairs with the compete / cooperate spectrum earlier in this deck.

ERP of record

SAP, Oracle, Workday, NetSuite record the event. They were not built to analyze across systems, forecast from operations, or host AI.

Planning & close tools

Anaplan, Adaptive, Planful, BlackLine, FloQast, OneStream, and Excel keep their seats. Feed cleaner actuals and drivers; do not ask FP&A to abandon the surface they trust.

Regulated function

Governance is the architecture: lineage, access, audit logs — the same posture banks and insurers already buy. Controls first, then speed.

When they ask for hard ROI, translate to days and working capital — then bring the customer proof, not a feature tour.
