## Slide 6 — Why SOX exists (Enron framing)

**Time:** ~2 min  
**Layout:** Broadsheet clipping facsimile + four-step cause/effect arc

### On-slide content
**Headline:** Why SOX exists

**Clipping (stylized, illustrative — never presented as a real publication):** masthead line `Front page · illustrative facsimile` · `December 2001` · headline **Enron collapses** · subdeck “Accounting fraud. Restated results. Numbers no one could trust.” · greeked columns · caption naming it an illustration.

**Arc (four steps, labels + short phrases only):**
1. **2001 · Fraud exposed** — The reported numbers were false.
2. **2002 · Sarbanes-Oxley** — US federal law, enforced by the SEC.
3. **Since · Controls, not stickers** — Executives personally certify the numbers.
4. **Your deal · Controller · Audit · CFO** — “Is this inside our control environment?”

No lead paragraph, no stamp: the clipping carries the story and the explanation lives in the notes.

### Speaker notes
**Purpose:** Give non-finance sellers a one-minute mental model of SOX before the compare and the answer — carried by the visual, not by slide copy.

**Say this:**  
Quick show of hands — who has heard of Enron? Yeah. This is the front page every Controller and CFO spends a career avoiding — and the clipping is illustrative, not a real paper. Enron collapsed in 2001 because the reported numbers were false, and investors, employees, and auditors all found out too late. Congress answered in 2002 with Sarbanes-Oxley: a US federal law, enforced by the SEC, that makes public-company executives personally accountable for the integrity of the numbers they report. They sign. Their name is on it. So SOX is not a vendor sticker — it is documented change management, access, testing, and evidence that a reported number can be defended, including evidence about the systems that produced it. That is why the last step matters to you. In your deals the Controller, Internal Audit, and the CFO are asking one question about Genie and the lakehouse: is this inside our control environment? Most of you sell HIPAA fluently. SOX is the finance cousin you may never have been asked to name out loud.

**Key nuance:** Keep Enron to a tight opener — educational, not a comedy bit.

**Accuracy:** Enron’s 2001 collapse and SOX passing in 2002 are factual. The clipping is stylized — no fake publication name, no attributed quote. No Databricks compliance claims here; those live on the exact-answer slide.

**Transition:** You already know HIPAA. Here’s how SOX is a different question.

You’re fluent in HIPAA. Protected health information, privacy and security buyers, BAAs, access and de-identification. SOX is a different animal. ## Slide 8 — SOX: the exact answer

**Time:** ~4 min  
**Layout:** Document facsimile + never-say list  
**Source:** Chris’s standard SOX slide (QuidelOrtho deck appendix)

### On-slide content
**Headline:** Databricks supports SOX-compliant workloads; no cloud provider holds a SOX certification

**DO say (primary answer — keep on slide, scannable):**  
- Databricks **can support a SOX-compliant workload**  
- Customers **already run** SOX-compliant workloads on Databricks  
- Most SOX requirements are about **how the customer** maintains source code and testing processes  
- Common patterns: **Repos** for source control · **Cluster policies** for allowed instance types / DBR versions  
- For review of Databricks itself → consult **SOC 2 Type II** controls for **change, release, and access management**

**NEVER say:**  
- “Databricks is SOX certified” / “We have SOX certification”  
- “SOX doesn’t apply to Genie”  
- “Don’t worry about Audit”

**Green callout (exact framing):**  
There is **no official SOX certification or validation** for cloud service providers. Databricks is a general-purpose, data-agnostic compute platform — not directly subject to SOX and not audited *for* SOX. We support customers’ compliance needs; point them to SOC 2 Type II for our controls.

### Speaker notes
**Purpose:** Install non-negotiable legal/positioning accuracy for the whole room.

**Say this:**  
This is the slide that saves deals. Commit it to memory. Short answer to “Is Databricks SOX compliant?”: **We support SOX-compliant workloads, and customers run them on us today.** Then immediately reframe: SOX is mostly about *their* change management, testing, access, and IPE — not a vendor sticker. We help them apply those practices on Databricks — Repos, cluster policies, Unity Catalog for governance, lineage, access. There is **no SOX certification for cloud providers**. If someone asks for our “SOX cert,” you say: **there isn’t one for any CSP — here’s our SOC 2 Type II, and here’s how we design the workload so *your* controls operate.** I have given this talk with Controllership and Audit in the room — QuidelOrtho is an example. Wrong words lose trust instantly. Right words make you the adult in the room.

**Key nuance:**  
- “SOX Ready” on marketing certification maps ≠ “SOX certified.” Don’t blur those.  
- Offer to bring Audit into the workshop; never bypass them.  
- Full verbatim paragraph in Appendix A for handout / Slack.

**Transition:** Saying the words isn’t enough — how you *run* the meeting.

## Slide 9 — How to run the SOX conversation

**Time:** ~4 min  
**Layout:** Control-need → Databricks pattern matrix + SoD strip

### On-slide content
**Headline:** Run SOX as their control environment on Unity Catalog, with Audit in the room

**Control map:** Change mgmt · Access / SoD · Evidence trail · Config lock-down → Repos/CI/CD, UC entitlements, UC audit/lineage, cluster policies.

**Bottom SoD strip:** `Requestor → Approver → Grantor → Auditor`  
**Room:** Controller · Internal Audit · FinTrans · Platform/IT — workshop, not a stealth Genie pilot.

### Speaker notes
**Purpose:** Turn positioning into a meeting plan SAs/AEs can run.

**Say this:**  
When SOX comes up, don’t argue product. Run this playbook. One: reframe — we’re not claiming a cert; we’re showing how *their* control environment runs on Databricks. Two: put Unity Catalog in the center — lineage, access, audit logs — and pair with Repos, cluster policies, CI/CD. That’s what I walked Controllership through at QuidelOrtho. Three: get Audit in the workshop. A Genie pilot that skips Audit becomes a Genie ban later. Bottom strip — segregation of duties. In large enterprises access is Requestor, Approver, Grantor, Auditor — often via SailPoint or an in-house EMS. Your discovery question: *“How do you grant warehouse access today, and which system owns SoD?”* If you can’t answer that, you’re not ready to put finance gold in Genie.

**Discovery questions (teach aloud):**  
- What is used for entitlement / access management? Who owns it?  
- How does that process grant access to warehouses today?  
- Is lakehouse / Genie already in SOX scope, or will OneERP / finance models pull it in?

**Transition:** Trust and SOX get you through the meeting. Timing gets you the meeting.

## Appendix A — Exact SOX verbiage (handout / Slack)

*From Chris’s standard slide — use verbatim when precision matters:*

> The Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 (SOX) is a US federal law administered by the United States Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). Databricks can support a SOX-compliant workload, and Databricks has customers that are running SOX-compliant workloads that leverage the Databricks platform. Most SOX requirements are about how customers maintain their source code, and their testing processes. Most requirements that customers ask from Databricks are how they can apply those practices to their usage of Databricks. For example, we commonly see customers using the repos functionality to manage their source code, using cluster policies to implement specific allowed instance types and Databricks Runtime versions.
>
> When it comes to actual audits, there is no SOX certification or validation for cloud service providers. As Databricks is a general-purpose data-agnostic compute platform and the product is not subject to regulations like SOX, Databricks does not provide any software or services that are audited for SOX. That said, we are happy to support customers with their SOX Compliance needs. For review of Databricks itself, we recommend that customers consult the controls within our SOC 2 Type II report that cover core requirements surrounding change management, release management, and access management.
